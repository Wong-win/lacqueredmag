The bathroom has, in the contemporary home, become, in our view, the most under-designed room. The bathroom is, in most cases, the room that the contemporary renovation industry has been most aggressively *over-specifying* — with hard surfaces, cold tile, chrome, *spa-grade* fittings that, in most cases, the wearer does not need. The bathroom is also the room the contemporary wearer is, in most cases, *most under-using* — a *pass-through*, not a *destination*.

This essay is, in part, a counter-argument to the over-specifying. It is also an argument that the bathroom is, properly understood, a *beauty ritual space* — where the wearer, in the morning or the evening, *does the work* of being looked at. The bathroom is the room where the *lacquered life* is, in fact, *assembled*. The assembly is, in our view, what the bathroom is for.

The five elements below are, in our view, the elements that, in the most consistent way, *make* the bathroom into a beauty ritual space. They are not, in most cases, *expensive* — they are *architectural*. They are not *renovations* — they are *additions*. They are not *decorations* — they are *instruments*. The instruments are what the bathroom is for.

## One — the dedicated bathing mirror with task light

The first element is a dedicated bathing mirror, with task light. The dedicated bathing mirror is, in our view, the most under-installed object in the contemporary bathroom. The bathroom mirror, in most cases, is the *only* mirror in the bathroom — *fixed* above the sink. The fixed mirror, in most cases, *cannot* be moved, tilted, or used while the wearer is in the bath.

The dedicated bathing mirror is, in our view, a *separate* mirror — on a *stand*, a *suction mount*, or a *small shelf*. It is *positioned* at the bath — close enough that the wearer, in the bath, can *see* herself. It is, in most cases, *small* — a hand mirror that can be held, or a small standing mirror placed on a stool.

The task light is the *partner* of the dedicated mirror — a *small* light, a clip light or a small LED panel, that can be *directed* at the wearer, in the bath, in a way the overhead bathroom light *cannot*. The task light is the light that, in the bath, allows the wearer to *see* the work she is doing — the mask, the hair treatment, the *bath ritual* that the contemporary bathroom *does not support*.

The dedicated bathing mirror, with task light, is, in our view, the element that *most transforms* the bathroom into a beauty ritual space — and the one that is *least expensive* to install.

## Two — the single tray for in-shower oils

The second element is a single tray for in-shower oils. The tray is, in our view, the most *under-designed* bathroom object. The tray is, in most cases, the *only* object that the contemporary bathroom has for the *holding* of the in-shower products — the oils, the masks, the cleansers, the *apothecary* of the shower. The tray is, in most cases, *plastic*, *wire*, or *chrome* — the materials the contemporary bathroom industry has, in our view, *most over-used*.

The dedicated tray is, in our view, a *single* tray — one that holds *only* what the wearer is, today, *using*. The single tray is the tray that, in the bathroom, *most supports* the daily ritual — the tray that, in the morning, holds the cleanser and the body oil, and that, in the evening, holds the in-shower mask and the in-bath treatment.

The single tray is, in our view, *teak* or *stone* — the materials the contemporary bathroom industry has, in our view, *most forgotten*. The teak is *water-resistant*. The stone is *durable*.

The single tray is, in our view, the element that *most supports* the daily ritual — and the one that is *most worth investing in*.

## Three — the hook for the bath sheet

The third element is a hook for the bath sheet. The bath sheet is, in our view, the most *over-looked* bathroom object. The bath sheet is, in most cases, the *largest* towel in the bathroom — the towel that the wearer, in most cases, *wraps* around the body, after the bath or after the shower. The bath sheet is, in most cases, *hung* on a hook — a hook that, in most cases, is *behind* the bathroom door, or *over* the radiator, or *on* the back of the bathroom door.

The hook for the bath sheet is, in our view, a *dedicated* hook. The dedicated hook is, in our view, a hook that is *positioned* at the bath — close enough to the bath that the wearer, in the bath, can *reach* the bath sheet without *standing*. The dedicated hook is, in our view, a hook that is *strong* — a hook that, in most cases, *holds* the bath sheet when the bath sheet is *wet*.

The dedicated hook is, in our view, also a hook that is *beautiful* — a hook that, in our view, is, in most cases, *brass* or *blackened* steel, the materials that the contemporary bathroom industry has, in our view, *most over-looked*. The dedicated hook is, in our view, the hook that, in the bathroom, *most rewards* the wearer, in the bath, who, in the bath, *reaches* for the bath sheet.

The hook for the bath sheet is, in our view, the element that, in the contemporary bathroom, *most rewards* the wearer. The hook is, in our view, also the element that, in the contemporary bathroom, is *least expensive* to install. The hook is, in our view, also the element that, in the contemporary bathroom, *most improves* the bath ritual.

## Four — the stool for the in-bath treatments

The fourth element is a stool for the in-bath treatments. The stool is, in our view, the most *under-installed* object in the contemporary bathroom — the bathroom has, in most cases, *no* place for the wearer to *sit*, except the *toilet*. The contemporary bathroom has, in most cases, *no* place for the *bathing rituals* that the wearer, in our view, *needs* — the in-bath treatment, the in-bath mask, the in-bath *waiting*.

The stool is, in our view, a *small* stool — *teak* or *stone*, *low* enough that the wearer, in the bath, can *rest* her arm on the stool, in a way that the *arm-only* balancing of the bath edge, in most cases, *does not allow*.

The stool is, in our view, the element that, in the contemporary bathroom, *most supports* the in-bath ritual — the element that *most rewards* the wearer who, in the bath, *needs* a place to *rest*. The rest is, in our view, what the in-bath ritual is *for*.

## Five — the stack of three towels

The fifth element is a stack of three towels, within reach. The stack of three towels is, in our view, the most *under-stocked* bathroom object. The contemporary bathroom has, in most cases, *one* towel — the towel that the wearer, in most cases, *uses*, in the morning, after the shower. The contemporary bathroom has, in most cases, *no* stack — the contemporary bathroom has, in most cases, *no* place for the *multiple* towels that the wearer, in the bath ritual, *needs*.

The stack of three is, in our view, *three* towels — a *bath sheet*, a *hand towel*, and a *face cloth*. The stack is, in our view, *positioned* at the bath — close enough to the bath that the wearer, in the bath, can *reach* any of the three towels, without *standing*. The stack is, in our view, *folded* — the stack is, in most cases, *folded* in a way that the *top* towel is, in most cases, the *first* towel the wearer *reaches*.

The stack of three is, in our view, the element that, in the contemporary bathroom, *most completes* the bath ritual. The stack is, in our view, also the element that, in the contemporary bathroom, is *least expensive* to install. The stack is, in our view, also the element that, in the contemporary bathroom, *most rewards* the wearer, in the bath, who, in the bath, *needs* the *three* towels.

---

The five elements are, in sum: a dedicated bathing mirror with task light, a single tray for in-shower oils, a hook for the bath sheet, a stool for the in-bath treatments, and a stack of three towels within reach. The five elements are, in our view, the elements that, in the most consistent way, *make* the bathroom into a beauty ritual space.

The spa is, in our view, not a luxury. The spa is, in our view, the *architecture of slowness*. The architecture is, in our view, what the bathroom has, in the last thirty years, *most lost*. The architecture is, in our view, what the five elements are, finally, *recovering*.

The recovery is, in our view, the most interesting *interior design* project the contemporary home can take on. The recovery is, in our view, also the most *useful* project — the project that, in our view, the contemporary wearer, in our view, *most needs*. The need is, in our view, finally, what the bathroom is for.
